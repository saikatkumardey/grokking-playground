grokking, from scratch, in the browser

a neural network memorizes, then suddenly understands

let's say you show a neural network half the answers to an addition table (a + b mod p). it memorizes them perfectly. train accuracy 100%, test stays near zero. looks like it just memorized, right?

then you keep training. for a long time nothing seems to happen. and then, thousands of steps later, test accuracy jumps from near-zero to near-perfect. the network figured out the rule, not just the answers. that's grokking.

why does it happen? the general rule turns out to be a handful of sine waves (Nanda et al. 2023), which is a cheaper way to fit the data than a lookup table of every pair. weight decay penalizes expensive solutions, so it tips the network toward the rule. the hidden progress chart tracks how much the network relies on those sine waves.

you can try an MLP or a Transformer (the original paper used a Transformer). both are written from scratch in javascript. the two knobs that matter most are weight decay and training fraction. try the presets below.

Problem

The network learns a + b mod p. Primes are traditional (Power et al. use 97), but addition works for any modulus. Not a prime: still works for addition, but results won't match the paper.
How much of all (a, b) pairs it gets to train on. The rest are held out.
Single hidden layer size.

Regularization

Shrinks every weight a little each step, favoring the cheap general solution over the expensive lookup table. Too little and it memorizes forever. Too much and it can't even memorize.
Step size for gradient descent.

Speed

Same seed + same settings = same run. Applied on reset.

Hidden progress what's happening during the "flat" part

fourier concentration = share of the network's weight energy in its top 5 frequencies (random weights sit near 5 / (p/2)). when it rises, the network is building the sine-wave solution; compare its timing with test accuracy. weight norm = sum of squared weights. try toggling weight decay and watch how differently it grows. dashed lines: grey = memorized (train ≥ 99%) and white = grokked (test ≥ 90%). the gap between them is the grokking delay.

Fourier spectrum what frequencies the network uses

each bar = one frequency component (k=1 to p/2). after grokking, only a few bars should spike. those are the frequencies that solve modular addition.
